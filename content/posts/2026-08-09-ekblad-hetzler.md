The Norwegian artist Ida Ekblad's friend has a young child who mispronounces her name as "Eatan Eggplant". _EAT AN EGGPLANT_ it is, then. A suitable title, then, for this show, and an artist whose work is full of things bleeding into other things.

It's her first show in the gallery's London space, though her sixth with Max Hetzler overall. Two rooms, and each one bleeds differently.

[!Ida Ekblad: EAT AN EGGPLANT (installation view)](/ekblad-hetzler.jpeg)

The dark second gallery holds a large glass lantern, spinning slowly, pendant-hung, an LED glowing inside it. Ekblad has cross-hatched the glass with thick, shiny paint on top, so the light comes through broken up, in fragments.

The other room is louder: oil on linen, enamel on steel, earthy and gestural. Strong, a bit brutal. There's an obvious debt to Dubuffet in the chunky biomorphic forms and the crude figuration, though Ekblad's palette is deeper than Dubuffet's chalky ochres.

At the end of the main gallery a long table holds two glass benches and three hand-painted bronze sculptures, arranged like place settings. Paper plates sit on the table too, painted over, laid out as if for a meal nobody's going to eat. Delicate lace elsewhere in the show turns up here recast in heavy bronze. Same motif, different weight each time.

_Ida Ekblad: EAT AN EGGPLANT is at Galerie Max Hetzler (London). 3 June - 8 August 2026_
