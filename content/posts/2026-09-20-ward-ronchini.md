Rebecca Ward makes all her works by herself, in her garage in Brooklyn. Some of the newer ones have been brought together in a [London](https://www.ronchinigallery.com/current/) [exhibition](https://www.ronchinigallery.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ArtPlugged.pdf), which opened before midsummer, hence the title. I know this, because, clocking the obviously labour intensive process that goes in to making her works, I asked the gallerist if she had any assistants? The answer - no, but I wish she would.

The paintings - or maybe sculptures? - are made up of meticulously cut and sewed together pieces of canvas, each coloured slightly differently, which are then pulled over the wooden canvas stretchers. They share a palpable hollowness and fragility. This latest set, at Ronchini, often feature a feathery-looking bottom or side edge, like the fringe of a scarf. Looking closer I could see that these edges too are made up of minutely, precisely cut up and stuck together shards of canvas. All this is made by one person?

![Rebecca Ward, ‘burgundy moon’ (2025)](/ward-ronchini-1.jpg)
_Rebecca Ward, ‘burgundy moon’ (2025)_

The works are symphonic. Their colours and shapes club together, forming an abstract image, a flow of moods. A little Rothko, a little Sean Sculley: both master conductors of this kind of thing. Though Ward’s stitched-together fabrics are also somehow softer, more down home.

They’re also obviously products of an uncompromisingly exacting mind and artistic vision. While I wish she shared the hours of labour to product her works, I suppose Ward doesn’t want other people in her space, interfering with her obsession. Anyway, the gallerist also told me she’s sold all but one of the works on show. Understandably.

_Rebecca Ward: lighter later is at Ronchini (London). 11 June - 30 September 2026_
